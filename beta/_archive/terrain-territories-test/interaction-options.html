50 ways to make the atlas easier to move through

Ten changes implemented; forty further possibilities. These proposals come from an interface audit. They do not revise the film archive, Indigenous affiliations or source-map shapes.

Implemented in this pass

  1. A–Z selection flies to its geography

    Selecting a filmmaker fits all available mapped land references. If none exist, it fits the recorded country. A film selection also locates its credited record when the filmmaker changes.

  2. Show what the camera means

    A restrained map readout names the filmmaker and distinguishes affiliation references from a country-only fallback. Missing geography stays unplaced; no residence or coordinate is invented.

  3. Make camera movement interruptible

    Short, bounded transitions are interrupted by a new selection, return or clear. Hover never pans. Reduced-motion preference makes camera changes immediate.

  4. Return to exactly where browsing stopped

    Back/Close from a filmmaker restores the originating letter, query, filters, result limit, scroll and camera; keyboard focus returns to the selected result. Film closing restores its page and result focus.

  5. Previous and next within the actual results

    A compact profile rail gives previous/next names and position. Filmmakers use the originating result sequence, so an A–Z or country browse stays coherent. Films use the current filtered film results.

  6. Give Clear, Overview and Reset distinct jobs

    Clear removes the selection while retaining search, filters and camera. Overview changes only the camera. Reset returns the entire interface to its quiet opening and retains saved views.

  7. Make Escape back out one layer

    Escape closes an open panel, then a profile to its results, then a search, then a place selection, then the alphabet browse. It never invokes Reset all.

  8. Expose small, reversible search exits

    Clear search appears synchronously while typing. The chosen A–Z letter becomes a removable chip. Empty results offer Clear search and, when applicable, Clear filters.

  9. Let the empty map return to results

    Clicking empty water or background closes the profile to its list. Land and country targets retain their own selection behavior. Hover stays temporary and leaves the selected camera intact.

  10. Connect phone profiles and maps explicitly

    Show map fits the current reference and takes a phone user to it. Map/Profile jump labels reflect the selection, preserve context and provide the return route without automatically scrolling away from a newly opened profile.

Further possibilities

  1. Preview the destination in each result

    A short land/country subtitle could indicate where selection will move the camera.

  2. Offer a map-follow toggle

    Frequent comparison users could hold the camera while changing selected records.

  3. Offer fit-one versus fit-all affiliations

    For multiple land links, a compact choice could focus one reference without losing the complete set.

  4. Show offscreen affiliation directions

    Small edge indicators could reveal that another selected reference is outside the current view.

  5. Offer a regional orientation inset

    A small inset could maintain continental orientation during deep zoom.

  6. Keep a short place breadcrumb

    Americas → country reference → land reference could provide camera navigation without redefining affiliation.

  7. Add a return-to-last-camera action

    One camera undo could reverse an accidental zoom without undoing the selection.

  8. Separate selected and preview styling further

    A clear solid selected treatment and lighter preview could reduce ambiguity in dense land overlaps.

  9. Improve keyboard map panning

    Arrow-key pan and plus/minus zoom could operate while the map has keyboard focus.

  10. Add a precise overlap chooser near the pointer

    A small menu could let users choose among overlapping lands without crossing to the side panel.

  11. Let A–Z group by displayed name or surname

    An explicit preference could support different research habits while preserving full names.

  12. Add a single jump-to-letter field

    A compact keyboard-friendly letter selector could supplement the full alphabet on phones.

  13. Offer true filmmaker pagination

    Optional 20-entry pages could replace Show more for users who need predictable list length.

  14. Show selected results without hiding their neighbors

    A split compact list/detail mode could support rapid scanning on large screens.

  15. Add compare-two profiles

    A temporary comparison could show recorded affiliations and works alongside one another.

  16. Pin a short temporary reading queue

    A few records could be held for exploration without saving whole views.

  17. Keep independent scroll per tab and per query

    Returning to a query could restore its prior result location, beyond the current selection return.

  18. Support keyboard result traversal

    Up/down in a focused list could move result focus while Enter selects and moves the map.

  19. Improve search interpretation hints

    Quiet suggestions could distinguish matching person names, film titles and land references.

  20. Offer typo-tolerant suggestions after no exact results

    Suggestions should be visibly approximate and never merge identities.

  21. Add explicit country/land scope breadcrumbs

    A one-line breadcrumb could explain why the current filmmaker set is limited.

  22. Provide a scope-only clear action

    Release the place scope while retaining date/language filters and the active query.

  23. Show filter impact before applying it

    Counts beside language/form options could clarify how many results would remain.

  24. Offer an undo after clearing

    A temporary Undo could restore the previous selection or filter state.

  25. Make zero-result combinations explain themselves

    A concise explanation could identify the country, date or language intersection responsible.

  26. Let URLs encode the visible camera

    Copied links could carry a bounded camera position, in addition to selection and filter state.

  27. Label browser-local saves more clearly

    Saved view rows could summarize their query, layers and place at a glance.

  28. Let users rename saved views

    Short custom titles could make a large saved collection readable.

  29. Export and import saved views

    A small JSON export could move a personal browsing collection between devices.

  30. Warn before a meaningful unsaved replacement

    When replacing a named saved view, show the specific pending change rather than a generic confirmation.

  31. Offer a compact map legend that follows context

    The legend could show only the currently visible selected/preview/source styles.

  32. Differentiate country totals from filtered counts

    A tiny two-part count could show full catalog total and current result count without changing the total.

  33. Scale label prominence within a readable range

    Small lands with many films could be enunciated without filling the map with giant text.

  34. Improve label collision at middle zoom

    Priority could favor the selection and nearby references without dropping country-total access.

  35. Introduce a deliberate focus halo

    A restrained selected-area wash could improve orientation without turning a people into a pin.

  36. Offer a low-distraction reading mode

    Hide unselected labels temporarily while keeping land color and selected traces.

  37. Add a persistent mobile split-view choice

    Users could choose stacked or map-led exploration rather than scrolling long distances.

  38. Make panel widths resizable on desktop

    A bounded draggable divider could balance reading and spatial exploration.

  39. Measure actual input latency on older devices

    Profile selection, hover and search could be tuned using timings rather than extra visual effects.

  40. Add a concise first-use interaction guide

    A dismissible three-step explanation could show preview, select and return using the actual interface.
