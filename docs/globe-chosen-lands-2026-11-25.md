# Globe: chosen lands, local land, no satellite (25 November 2026)

John's decision (25 Nov): keep the sphere, drop what made it a surveillance view.

- **No satellite imagery, no elevation.** NASA GIBS, EOxCloudless and Mapterhorn are removed from the globe. Land is the Natural Earth 1:50m coastline (`assets/world-50m.json`, already local) filled in the flat map's stone `#d9dbdd`; the sea is the flat map's blue-grey `#8093a1`; rivers and lakes from OpenFreeMap at zoom 6+, in the same colour. Basemap place, water and peak names stay off, as on the flat map. The Terrain (tilted) button is gone; `syncTerrain` does nothing.
- **Territories at full strength.** Ambient fill 0.30–0.40, edges 0.35–0.70 (Soft/Strong still multiply these). Lands are always on: the Lands switch is hidden and an old `territories=off` link opens with lands shown.
- **No reticle, no tuning.** `#globe-aperture` is hidden (kept in the page because hit-testing measures it). Moving the globe never chooses a land. A land opens when it is chosen: click or tap it, pick it in Find, follow a link, or press Enter (the land at the centre of the view). It stays open while the globe moves, until another is chosen (`heldLand` in `settle()`); Home clears it. With nothing chosen the panel is hidden (`#globe-encounter.is-empty`). Hovering shows the land's name (`#globe-hover-name`).
- **Globe, then flat.** Projection `['interpolate',['linear'],['zoom'],5,'vertical-perspective',7,'mercator']`: a sphere at the scale of the continent, a flat map at the scale of a land.
- **Antimeridian.** The native land fill uses the unmerged countries, with rings made continuous past ±180 and polar rings closed along the pole (`planarRings`). Without this Chukotka and Antarctica drew bands round the globe (previously hidden under the satellite layer).
- **Fallback canvas** uses the same stone, blue-grey and ink; the colour relief raster is no longer drawn.
- `assets/globe-experiment.js` (unused) is deleted.

Checks changed to the new contract: `tools/qa/globe-independent.py` (terrain mode: no remote imagery or mesh, reticle hidden, chosen land held across camera moves; lands always on), `tools/qa/globe-discovery.py`, `tools/qa/globe-share.py`. Local runs (Chromium, SwiftShader, external hosts blocked): terrain, input, discovery-page and share suites passed; the independent discovery mode stops on an artwork-frame check locally (images blocked), as the unedited code also fails locally.

Still to do: Constellate on the globe (night sky, threads as arcs), the theme web as arcs, then retire `flat.html`.

## Near the poles (26 Nov, 6 pm, beta only)
John: "Little jumpy in the poles when moving around." Reproduced on the live beta in his Chrome and locally.
- **What happened.** Dragged north from the opening view, the centre reached 90 degrees and passed over the pole: the longitude flipped (from 83 W to 93 E in one drag) and the view pinned there, turning instead of moving. Any zoom near a pole made the Earth lurch: the map library lowers its zoom toward the poles to hold the Earth's size (2.34 at the opening view, 0.8 at 70 N, below zero at the pole), while the atlas clamped and converted zoom as if latitude did not matter, so one wheel notch at 70 N more than doubled the Earth.
- **The centre stops at 79 degrees** north and south, through the library's own constraint (`setTransformConstrain`), so a drag runs into it without being cut off. Every land can still be centred: the northernmost point is Avanersuarmiut at 77.8 N. The poles stay in view.
- **The atlas's zoom is now the Earth's apparent size at every latitude** (`scaleOf`, `mapZoomFor`, `minMapZoom`): dragging from the opening view to 79 N keeps it at 1.00 (it read 0.35 at 70 N before), and the wheel, the buttons, a chosen land and the least zoom behave there as they do at the equator.
- **Polar caps.** Web map imagery stops at 85 degrees, and the pole showed a pale disc. Two fills (`pole-caps`) carry the Arctic Ocean's washed colour and Antarctica's ice to the poles, with night colours.
- **Not changed:** near 79 degrees a sideways drag still turns the Earth quickly (the grabbed point stays under the pointer, and meridians are close together there), and imagery is coarser toward the poles because the library asks for lower-zoom tiles there.
- Local note: the sandbox's software renderer now and then loses its graphics context ("Graphics context lost" in `AtlasGlobe.liveFailure`); the globe then falls back to the plain canvas, as designed.
- `globe.js?v=60-plate`; a check in `tools/qa/globe-discovery.py`.
- **Shared views (v61).** The first push converted an arriving `at=` zoom by the old rule, so a shared view reopened 2% larger; Globe browser compatibility caught it on four of its jobs. The arrival now uses the same rule, and a view saved beyond 79 degrees opens at the limit.
- **Pushing against the limit (v62).** On the live beta a drag held against the limit still swung the Earth round the pole (91 W to 141 E over three drags). While a drag is under way the longitude is now held there too, so the Earth rests in place; a sideways drag along the limit still turns it.
- **Fast and coarse moves (v63).** On the live beta one long drag from 70 N still came out on the far side (91 W to 81 E): the library proposes a centre beyond the pole, on the opposite meridian. Any drag or flick whose proposed centre is past the limit, turns the longitude more than a quarter-turn at once, or lands more than 100 degrees of latitude away now stops at the limit on the meridian it was on. The atlas's own flights (`setCamera`, the overview handoff) are exempt and slide along the limit.
